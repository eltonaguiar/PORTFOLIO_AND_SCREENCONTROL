Notes on "proven" for a retail trader

"Proven" means the strategies show statistically significant positive risk-adjusted returns in peer-reviewed academic papers, survive out-of-sample tests by independent researchers, different geographies, longer histories, and alternative specifications - typically before or with modest institutional transaction costs.

It does not mean any of the following for retail traders:

You can reliably extract those returns net of your actual costs (retail spreads, commissions, slippage, and overnight financing are far worse than in the papers).
The edge survives publication, crowding, and changing market microstructure (many premia have attenuated post-publication).
You can tolerate the documented drawdowns (30-60%+ losses lasting years) without abandoning the strategy at the worst moment.
Easy implementation: most require futures, listed options, leverage, margin, 20-60 liquid instruments, low-cost execution, and tax-aware rebalancing that retail accounts rarely achieve.
Guaranteed positive expectancy - these are risk premia with crash risk, not arbitrage. The vast majority of self-directed retail traders lose money attempting systematic strategies.
Implementation at retail scale usually turns positive academic Sharpe ratios into flat or negative results after realistic frictions and behavior.
